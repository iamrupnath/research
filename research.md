**Research Date:**

**Market Covered:** West Bengal and India
**Main Areas:** Web Hosting, Domains and Business Email

---

# 1. Executive Summary

This report reviews the current market position of Caretel Webservice in two selected areas:

1. Web Hosting and related services
2. Professional / Business Email

The research covers Caretel's current website, product pages, terms, public competitor pricing, West Bengal-based competitors and larger Indian providers.

The main finding is that Caretel already has a clear technical service base. Its website talks about NVMe hosting, SSL, daily backups, 24/7 monitoring, managed migration, business email, anti-spam protection and support. Caretel also has a wider IT infrastructure background, which can support a more managed-service based business model.

The main gap is not the number of services. The bigger issue is how the services are presented and sold.

Many competitors already offer domain + hosting + email together. Several of them also show public prices, clear plan comparisons, online checkout, migration services, renewal prices and simple packages. QSS, HostKarle, BigRock and Hostinger are examples of this model.

For business email, Caretel currently positions itself as a premium managed service. Its public calculator shows ₹1,500 per month for 10 mailboxes with 5 GB storage per user, or ₹150 per mailbox per month. This is much higher than some basic business email providers. QSS lists 5 GB email at ₹450 per account per year, BigRock lists 5 GB email at ₹39 per account per month, and GoDaddy lists 10 GB email at ₹39 per mailbox per month for its first-year term.

This does not automatically mean that Caretel's price is too high. Caretel is trying to sell a managed service, not only mailbox storage. However, the website does not currently give enough measurable information to clearly explain the premium price.

The main opportunity is therefore to make the service easier to understand, compare and purchase, while keeping the managed-service model.

A possible direction is:

**Domain → Hosting → Business Email → Migration → Backup → Security → Monitoring → Support**

This would position Caretel as a managed digital infrastructure provider instead of competing only as another low-cost hosting company.

# 17. Domain Business Analysis

Caretel's public terms state that its portal provides domain availability search and that actual domain registration depends on separate agreements and GoDaddy's registry terms and pricing at the time of transaction.

This is important because the customer experience around domain registration needs to be clear.

A domain customer normally wants to understand:

* Registration price
* Renewal price
* Transfer price
* DNS management
* Domain ownership
* Domain expiry
* Privacy/protection options
* Nameserver management
* Domain transfer process

The Caretel public site currently does not provide the same level of visible pricing and plan information that larger domain platforms provide.

For comparison, the .IN Registry currently lists wholesale prices of ₹500 for a second-level .IN domain and ₹400 for third-level zones such as co.in, net.in and org.in. Registrars are free to set their own retail prices.

Therefore, if Caretel sells .IN domains, the retail offer should make the registration and renewal economics clear.

**Sales-assisted service for larger customers**

# 19. Major Gaps Found

## Gap 1: Hosting plans are not visible

The hosting page explains features but does not show clear packages, storage limits, number of websites, email limits or public prices.

## Gap 2: Email pricing is premium but the premium value is not fully explained

The current calculator shows ₹150/user/month at 5 GB for the 10-user example. This needs stronger support through measurable service features.

## Gap 3: Renewal pricing is not visible

Competitors such as BigRock and Hostinger clearly show promotional and renewal prices.

## Gap 4: Customer proof is limited

The website mentions more than 100 businesses and technical capabilities, but public case studies with customer name, problem, service and result are limited.

## Gap 5: Product bundles are not clearly presented

The natural combination of:

**Domain + Hosting + Email + SSL + Backup + Support**

is not presented as simple packages on the main public pages.

## Gap 6: Managed service value needs more detail

The site uses terms such as managed hosting, enterprise security and SLA support, but customers need more measurable details to understand the difference.

Examples could include:

* Support response time
* Backup retention
* Restore process
* Email migration scope
* SLA percentage
* Monitoring coverage
* Security controls
* Email security setup
* DNS management
* Account management

### Finding 2

### Finding 4

Caretel appears better suited to a managed-service position than a pure low-cost position.

Its website already focuses on managed deployment, migration, monitoring, support and infrastructure.

### Finding 5

The current website creates more friction for small customers.

The current flow depends heavily on enquiry and sales contact, while several competitors offer clear plans and online purchase.

### Finding 6

The largest visible gap is product clarity.

Customers need a simple answer to:

**What do I get?
How much does it cost?
What happens at renewal?
Why should I choose Caretel?**

These answers should be visible before the customer contacts sales.

# 34. Final Assessment

The research does not show a lack of services at Caretel.

Caretel already has a combination of hosting, email, infrastructure, monitoring and support capabilities.

The main issue is how these capabilities are converted into simple products that customers can understand and buy.

The market is divided into two broad groups:

**Low-cost, self-service providers**

and

**Managed, support-led providers**

Caretel's current website is closer to the second group.

The opportunity is therefore to make the managed-service value much clearer while adding simple entry-level products for customers who do not need a full consultation.

A possible positioning direction is:

**“Domain, Website, Business Email and Managed Infrastructure under one technical team.”**

This would allow Caretel to sell more than hosting space or email storage. It would create a connected service model where one customer can buy multiple products and remain with the company as the business grows.
